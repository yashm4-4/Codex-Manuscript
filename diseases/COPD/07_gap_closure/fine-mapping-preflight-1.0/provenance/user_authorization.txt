Begin a new versioned COPD V2 fine-mapping and risk-direction preflight only, e.g. fine-mapping-preflight-1.0.
This stage is a data-access, phenotype, statistical-prerequisite and LD feasibility audit.
Do not perform statistical fine-mapping yet.
The purpose is to determine which COPD GWAS datasets, ancestries and loci can support scientifically defensible fine-mapping and signed disease-risk interpretation.
Earlier V2 stages are immutable.
Do not score V2 models.
Do not score or rerank the 337 V1 candidates.
Do not use candidate membership to select GWAS datasets or loci.
Do not perform eQTL colocalization or target-gene analysis yet.
Do not revise the manuscript.
1. Start from the frozen phenotype hierarchy
Use the completed COPD phenotype-robustness classification as the biological hierarchy.
Distinguish at minimum:
- primary direct COPD susceptibility: clinically/spirometrically defined COPD case-control association;
- secondary EHR/PheCode/ICD COPD or airway-obstruction phenotypes;
- ML-derived COPD/liability phenotypes;
- COPD severity/progression/decline within cases;
- chronic bronchitis or related COPD subphenotypes;
- smoking-interaction analyses;
- lung-function traits that are not themselves COPD.
Direct COPD susceptibility should be preferred for the manuscript's primary causal-genetic analysis when statistically feasible.
Do not combine these phenotype classes into one fine-mapping analysis.
2. Audit all potentially usable GWAS studies
Begin from the frozen 104-study core COPD inventory and the existing phenotype audit.
For every study/accession that could plausibly support locus-level fine-mapping, verify rather than infer:
- accession;
- publication;
- phenotype definition;
- phenotype class above;
- cohort(s);
- ancestry or ancestries;
- cases/controls or total N;
- whether the result is ancestry-specific or a trans/multi-ancestry meta-analysis;
- genome build;
- variant identifier format;
- REF/ALT or effect/non-effect allele fields;
- effect allele;
- OR/log(OR)/beta;
- SE;
- P value;
- allele frequency if available;
- per-variant N if available;
- imputation/variant-quality fields if available;
- whether statistics are genome-wide/locus-complete versus significant associations only;
- current public access URL/source;
- whether unrestricted download is actually possible;
- exact source-file/header evidence supporting these claims.
Do not treat the GWAS Catalog field indicating that summary statistics exist as proof that the file is usable for fine-mapping.
Where appropriate, inspect public file headers and representative records to verify schema and signedness.
Internet/public-resource lookup is authorized for this preflight, but retain URLs, access dates and checksums for any downloaded metadata or files.
3. Explicitly audit the strongest direct-COPD anchors
Give particular attention to direct COPD case-control studies including the Sakornsakolpat et al. 2019 COPD GWAS (GCST007692) and any other direct-COPD study for which full signed statistics may now be publicly obtainable.
Determine whether complete locus-wide/meta-analysis summary statistics can actually be obtained.
If they cannot:
- say so explicitly;
- identify whether author/consortium request would be required;
- do not reconstruct the study from GWAS Catalog significant hits;
- do not substitute another phenotype while continuing to call it the Sakornsakolpat COPD analysis.
4. Evaluate technically tractable public alternatives without promoting them automatically
Audit technically tractable resources such as UK Biobank/Pan-UKB, GBMI, MVP, BioBank Japan or other COPD resources only where relevant.
For each, distinguish:
- phenotype fidelity to COPD;
- ancestry;
- availability of signed complete statistics;
- availability and compatibility of cohort-matched or closely matched LD;
- cohort overlap with other analyses;
- whether it is suitable for primary, secondary sensitivity, or not suitable use.
A technically convenient EHR or ML phenotype must not silently replace a better direct-COPD phenotype.
5. Risk/effect-allele direction contract
Establish the exact rule that any future stage must use to define the disease-increasing allele.
Disease-risk direction may come only from a valid signed association result, e.g.:
- positive log(OR)/beta for the encoded effect allele;
- OR > 1 for the effect allele;
- if OR < 1, the opposite allele is disease-increasing after verified allele harmonization.
Explicitly prohibit inferring the risk allele from:
- REF versus ALT;
- major/minor allele;
- allele frequency;
- ancestral/derived state;
- LD correlation alone;
- V1/V2 model direction;
- GTEx NES;
- nearest gene;
- biological expectation.
Record strand/allele harmonization rules and handling of palindromic SNPs, multi-allelic sites and indels.
6. LD feasibility audit
For each candidate GWAS dataset/ancestry, identify the best defensible LD source.
Preference order should reflect scientific matching rather than convenience:
1. cohort-matched LD where genuinely available;
2. ancestry-matched LD from a highly comparable source;
3. external ancestry reference only when justified and explicitly sensitivity-limited.
Do not automatically reuse the V1 1000 Genomes LD-proxy resource for fine-mapping.
For each possible LD source document:
- population/cohort;
- sample size;
- variant set;
- genome build;
- phasing/genotyping/imputation context;
- availability of signed LD/correlation rather than only r²;
- allele coding;
- access mechanism;
- compatibility with the summary statistics.
Flag mixed-ancestry meta-analysis statistics for which no defensible matching LD exists.
Do not use single-European LD for a multi-ancestry meta-analysis merely because it is available.
7. Define prospective fine-mapping eligibility gates
Before any fine-mapping is run, propose explicit gates that a study/locus must satisfy.
At minimum assess:
- phenotype suitability;
- complete locus-wide variant statistics;
- signed effect information;
- allele harmonization;
- adequate variant density;
- compatible genome build;
- sufficiently matched LD;
- signed summary-stat/LD allele consistency;
- acceptable missingness/variant overlap;
- valid sample-size information;
- LD-matrix QC;
- handling of multiple causal signals;
- handling of long-range/complex LD regions such as the MHC;
- ability to detect summary-statistic/LD inconsistencies.
Do not invent permissive thresholds merely to make a dataset pass. Provide literature/methodological justification for numerical gates that would materially affect inclusion.
8. Candidate/locus selection firewall
Future fine-mapping loci must be defined from the selected GWAS dataset's own association evidence, not from whether a region contains:
- a V1 337 candidate;
- a V2 model call;
- a known functional benchmark variant;
- a desired gene.
Record a prospective locus-definition strategy, for example independent genome-wide significant association regions with a prespecified genomic/LD boundary.
Do not inspect V2 candidate overlap when deciding which loci are technically eligible.
The purpose is to avoid selecting only loci where our prior model happens to look good.
9. Fine-mapping-method recommendation — design only
Based on the actual usable statistics and LD structures, recommend an appropriate future method or methods, such as a multi-signal summary-statistics approach.
Consider:
- SuSiE-RSS;
- FINEMAP;
- or another method if better justified.
Do not run them in this preflight.
Specify what inputs the chosen method requires, how multiple signals would be handled, how credible sets/PIPs would be interpreted, and what sensitivity analyses would be necessary.
Do not call a high-PIP variant biologically causal; statistical fine-mapping narrows association uncertainty.
10. Two-track outcome is acceptable
It is acceptable if the preflight concludes that:
- a high-quality direct-COPD dataset is suitable for primary fine-mapping;
- a public EHR/ICD COPD dataset is suitable only for secondary sensitivity;
- some historically important datasets cannot currently be fine-mapped defensibly.
Do not force every dataset into the analysis.
If no sufficiently defensible direct-COPD dataset is currently available, report that clearly rather than performing pseudo-fine-mapping from incomplete data.
11. Required outputs
Produce a versioned preflight bundle with:
- study-level phenotype/summary-stat eligibility matrix;
- verified source/accession/file inventory;
- header/schema audit;
- signed-effect/risk-allele feasibility table;
- ancestry/cohort table;
- study-overlap considerations;
- LD-source compatibility matrix;
- study-specific limitations;
- proposed fine-mapping eligibility gates;
- proposed locus-definition rules;
- recommended fine-mapping method(s);
- expected compute/storage requirements;
- list of datasets immediately executable;
- list requiring external access/request;
- list unsuitable for fine-mapping;
- explicit recommendation for the primary analysis dataset(s) and secondary sensitivity dataset(s);
- provenance/checksums for any newly acquired metadata/files;
- append-only activity/decision/result register updates;
- final preflight report and freeze.
12. Stop
Do not execute statistical fine-mapping.
At completion report:
- which direct-COPD datasets are actually fine-mappable now;
- which have valid signed risk direction;
- which have defensible matched LD;
- whether Sakornsakolpat 2019 is executable or requires external access;
- the best primary dataset;
- the best secondary/sensitivity dataset;
- the number of loci expected to be eligible;
- the proposed statistical method;
- all blockers that must be resolved before execution.
Then stop for investigator review.
